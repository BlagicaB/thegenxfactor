Why Your Network Is Your Moat After 50

Skills can be learned and tools change every year. What protects you is who you know, and who knows you.

By Blagica Bottigliero, founder · November 6, 2026

In business, a moat is the thing that protects you. It's what makes you hard to replace. For companies, it might be a brand or a patent. For Gen X women in today's job market, I believe the moat is something simpler: each other.

Skills aren't enough anymore

Most of us were taught that if you work hard and get good at your job, you'll be safe. Then 2008 happened. Then COVID. Now AI is reshaping entire departments, and middle managers are often the first to go.

Skills still matter. But tools change every year, and plenty of talented, experienced women are learning the hard way that being great at the job doesn't mean you keep it.

What lasts is relationships. The person who thinks of you when a fractional role opens up. The friend who says, "You should meet her." The former colleague who's building something and needs exactly what you know.

Why women need to build this on purpose

Men of our generation often built these networks without trying, on golf courses and in long-standing work circles. Many Gen X women were busy raising kids, caring for parents and doing the job, often all at once. We didn't have evenings free for networking drinks.

So the network has to be built on purpose. That's what The Gen X Factor is for.

What makes a network a moat

A big list of contacts isn't a moat. A moat is a network that's:

  • Trusted. Members fill out a real profile before they join, so you know who you're talking to.
  • Introduced. We match women by hand based on field, location and goals. More than nine in ten of our members have had at least one personal introduction.
  • Real-world. Members have started meeting in person, beginning in Charlotte with Metro Detroit next, and more meetups are being planned around the country.
  • Built on work. People come for connection and stay because it leads somewhere: gigs, partners, hires and new paths.

The compounding effect

The best part of a network is that it grows in value over time. Every woman who joins brings decades of experience, contacts and know-how. Every introduction makes the next one more likely to land.

That's the moat. Not a course you finish or a tool that changes next year, but a growing circle of women who know your work and want to see you win.

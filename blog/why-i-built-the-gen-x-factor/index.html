I'm Tired of Reading Reports About Us. So I Built Something.

Why I started The Gen X Factor, a place for Gen X women to find each other, work with each other and hire each other.

By Blagica Bottigliero, founder · November 12, 2026

The reports keep coming

If you're a Gen X woman, you've read the headlines. We're the sandwich generation, caring for aging parents while we raise kids and pay for college. We've been paid less than men for most of our careers. When companies cut, middle managers go first, and women go first among them. We saw it in 2008. We saw it again during COVID. And now there's a quieter kind of ageism. Nobody says it out loud, but you feel it in the room.

I'm grateful the conversation has finally opened up around perimenopause and menopause. That's real progress. But I'm sick of reading reports about us. Reports describe a problem. I wanted to build a solution.

The moment it clicked

A girlfriend of mine, a talented designer, told me she'd been pushed out of her job. Hearing her story, and how it happened, put me over the edge. She's brilliant and experienced, and suddenly she was on her own, wondering what came next.

I kept thinking: there are thousands of women like her. Why aren't we finding each other?

So on September 22, 2026, I started The Gen X Factor.

Who she is

The women who join are hard to put in one box, and that's the point. They're chiefs of staff and executives. They're television writers and people who built careers in Hollywood. They're middle managers and women who held the support roles that kept everything running.

Some were laid off. Some are still employed and quietly planning their next move. Some are freelancing or running their own businesses. What they share is this: for many of them, this work is all they've ever known. It was their livelihood and a big part of who they are. Starting over can feel unimaginable.

Why connection, and not another course

We're tired of digital courses. Some are good, but plenty of women see them as a sham, someone selling a dream back to us at a markup. What women want is practical help with this phase of life. How do I change careers? What does going fractional actually look like? Who's done it, and can I talk to her?

So many of us kept our heads down and did the job for decades. We never had time to look up and see what else was possible. Now big layoffs and buyout offers across tech, plus the speed of AI, are pushing a lot of women to look up all at once.

And let's be honest: not everyone is a natural tinkerer. Learning new tools and reinventing yourself at this stage can be frightening. That fear is easier to face next to women who understand it.

Proof it's real

Here's when I knew this was working. Women started meeting on Zoom. Then a group in Charlotte met up in person. Now get-togethers are being planned across the country, and I'm hosting one in Metro Detroit.

Once women saw this wasn't a fake community, that real people were connecting, something opened up. It fits a bigger pattern. Look at how many women are gathering over mahjong, game nights and dinners. The longevity research keeps coming back to one finding: community and human connection help us live longer and better. We need each other, and we're finally making time for it.

The next 30 years

Gen X women are walking into the next three decades carrying a lot at once: parents who need care, kids heading to college, the physical changes of perimenopause and menopause, and a job market that too often assumes a woman in midlife can't do the job.

That's complex. No app or course fixes it. But women who've been through it, or are going through it right now, can help each other through it.

In a year or two, I want The Gen X Factor to be full of success stories. Women who found a new purpose. Women who partnered with each other to build something. Women who changed careers or started businesses, using what other members learned along the way.

If you're feeling stuck

If you've been pushed out, or you can see it coming, or you just don't know what's next: you're not alone, and you're not done. There's a room full of women who get it. Come find them.

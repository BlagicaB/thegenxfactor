The Missing Room: Why Gen X Women Need a Network Built Around Work

There are wonderful spaces for Gen X women. None of them was built around what comes next at work.

By Blagica Bottigliero, founder · October 28, 2026

There are wonderful spaces for Gen X women right now. Newsletters that make us laugh. Facebook groups where we can vent about hot flashes and teenagers. Book clubs, podcasts, even cruises. I love that these exist. We earned every one of them.

But when a friend of mine was pushed out of her job, I looked around for where she should go next, and I couldn't find the room she needed. Not a place to commiserate. A place to work.

The question nobody was answering

When you lose a job in your 50s, or see it coming, the first feeling is grief. The second is a very practical question: what now?

Can I freelance? Who hires fractional people? Is anyone building something I could join? Has anyone changed careers at this age and lived to tell about it?

Those questions don't get answered in a group chat about '80s cereal. They get answered by women who've done it, and by women who need exactly the skills you have.

What our members told us

When women sign up for The Gen X Factor, we ask what they're looking for. The answers were striking. About three in four want freelance or fractional work. About the same number want to meet women who've changed careers. Most also want peers who simply get it.

A quarter of our members are between roles. A third are still employed and quietly planning their next move. Many others freelance or run their own businesses.

In other words, this is a working community. These women want friendship, and they also want a path.

Why this space was empty

I think it comes down to three things.

First, most career resources were built for people early in their careers. Job boards, bootcamps and networking events weren't designed with a 52-year-old chief of staff in mind.

Second, most midlife resources focus on lifestyle and health. That's important, and long overdue, but work often gets left out of the midlife conversation.

Third, many Gen X women spent decades with their heads down, doing the job. We never built the side network, because we never had time.

What we built instead

The Gen X Factor is a place where Gen X women hire each other, find fractional and freelance work, meet co-founders and connect with women who've already made the leap. Every new member gets introduced by hand to women who match her field, her location or her goals.

It's not a course. It's not a coaching program. It's a room full of women who've done the work, ready to help each other with what comes next.
